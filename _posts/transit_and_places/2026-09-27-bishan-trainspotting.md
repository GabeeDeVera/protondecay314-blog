## Introduction

![Platform Screen Door Signage at Bishan MRT]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/bishan-sign-1.jpg)
**Figure 1:** Platform Screen Door Signage at Bishan MRT

Just a few days ago, I celebrated my birthday. The week after my birthday, I had some exams to clear and interviews to attend to. Hence, as a birthday celebration and reward for completing a difficult week, I opted to do something I find fun and relaxing: **Trainspotting**.

*[Bishan MRT (NS17/CC15)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan_MRT_station)* was on top of the list of places I wanted to visit. The first time I rode a North-South Line train northbound, I was intrigued by how there was simultaneously sunlight shining outside the train window and full-height platform screen doors, something you would often find in underground stations. Bishan---specifically the North-South Line part---is unusual in that aspect: It is **at-grade**. It is neither elevated nor buried underground and sits quite close to ground level. In fact---I have confirmed this during my ride to visit all MRT stations---Bishan is the one and only train station on the entire MRT network that is at-grade. It will likely remain so in the foreseeable future, as the upcoming [Jurong Region Line (JRL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_Region_Line) and the [Cross-Island Line (CRL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Island_Line) are fully aboveground and underground, respectively.

Hence, unless the LTA decides to build sections of the [Tengah or Seletar lines (or the Tengah-Seletar line)](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2026/3/news-releases/next-phase-of-rail-development.html) partially at-grade (highly unlikely, in my opinion), Bishan will remain the sole title bearer of the **only at-grade station on the MRT network**.

There are plenty more interesting things to observe around Bishan station, but for now, let's begin our journey there!
