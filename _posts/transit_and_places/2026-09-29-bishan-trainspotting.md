## Introduction

![Platform Screen Door Signage at Bishan MRT]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/bishan-sign-1.jpg)
**Figure 1:** Platform Screen Door Signage at Bishan MRT

Just a few days ago, I celebrated my birthday. The week after my birthday, I had some exams to clear and interviews to attend to. Hence, as a birthday celebration and reward for completing a difficult week, I opted to do something I find fun and relaxing: **Trainspotting**.

*[Bishan MRT (NS17/CC15)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishan_MRT_station)* was on top of the list of places I wanted to visit. The first time I rode a North-South Line train northbound, I was intrigued by how there was simultaneously sunlight shining outside the train window and full-height platform screen doors, something you would often find in underground stations. Bishan---specifically the North-South Line part---is unusual in that aspect: It is **at-grade**. It is neither elevated nor buried underground and sits quite close to ground level. In fact---I have confirmed this during my ride to visit all MRT stations---Bishan is the one and only train station on the entire MRT network that is at-grade. It will likely remain so in the foreseeable future, as the upcoming [Jurong Region Line (JRL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_Region_Line) and the [Cross-Island Line (CRL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Island_Line) are fully aboveground and underground, respectively.

Hence, unless the LTA decides to build sections of the [Tengah or Seletar lines (or the Tengah-Seletar line)](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2026/3/news-releases/next-phase-of-rail-development.html) partially at-grade (highly unlikely, in my opinion), Bishan will remain the sole title bearer of the **only at-grade station on the MRT network**.

There are plenty more interesting things to observe around Bishan station, but for now, let's begin our journey there!

The area around Lakeside and Chinese Garden MRT also hosts dense residential areas next to expansive parks. Of note is the Jurong Lake and the surrounding Jurong Lake District.

I've arrived at Jurong East (EW24/NS1). Since Jurong East serves two (in the future, three) aboveground MRT lines---namely, the East-West (EWL), North-South (NSL), and, in the future, Jurong Region Lines (JRL)---the track layout seen here is some of the most complex on the entire MRT network. It is also a very busy station, especially during peak hours.

At Jurong East, I transfer onto the NSL. The station offers convenient cross-platform transfer between the EWL and NSL, a feature I really love. The EWL and NSL were planned together in the early stages of the MRT. The presence of cross-platform interchanges, the interoperability of the NSL and EWL tracks, and the sharing of rolling stock all demonstrate the systems-level planning in the MRT network.

I could talk a lot more about this topic, but for now, let's keep heading east towards Bishan.

## The Journey to Bishan: The North-South Line

Along the NSL, I passed by many Housing and Development Board (HDB) flats in areas such as Bukit Batok and Choa Chu Kang. As of September 2026, new housing units can be seen in construction around the Brickland area, found between Bukit Gombak (NS3) and Choa Chu Kang (NS4) stations. This site is the location of the future Brickland MRT (NS3A) infill station along the NSL.

Unfortunately, I cannot include a picture of them as they are quite close to a newly-declared protected area across Bukit Batok and Brickland roads.

{% include video.html url='assets/bishan-trainspotting/yew-tee-sungei-kadut-boundary-o.mp4' %}
**Figure 13:** Crossing Pang Sua River

Shortly after departing from Yew Tee MRT (NS5), we cross the Pang Sua river. This river demarcates the Yew Tee residential and the Sungei Kadut industrial areas. Sungei Kadut is also the site of a future infill station and interchange with the Downtown Line (DTL), [Sungei Kadut MRT (NS6/DE2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungei_Kadut_MRT_station).

![Crossing the Mandai River]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/mandai-river.jpg)
**Figure 14:** Crossing the Mandai River

Near Kranji MRT (NS7), we cross the Mandai River. This river is quite close to a [NEWater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEWater) facility, where Singapore treats wastewater and turns them into production-ready water. NEWater facilities are often marked Protected Areas, so please do avoid photographing them when possible.

{% include video.html url='assets/bishan-trainspotting/marsiling-o.mp4' %}
**Figure 15:** Back to Seeing HDBs

As we approach Marsiling MRT (NS8), we exit a short stretch of tunnels leading the NSL under the Woodlands Flyover and enter residential areas again. The alignment of the NSL takes us through dense residential neighbourhoods filled with HDBs, malls, and orderly road networks. In fact, it continues like this for quite a while until we reach Khatib MRT (NS14).

{% include video.html url='assets/bishan-trainspotting/lower-seletar-reservoir-o.mp4' %}
**Figure 16:** Lower Seletar Reservoir

Past Khatib, the NSL runs alongside the Lower Seletar Reservoir. This is perhaps one of the few places in Singapore where an aboveground MRT line passes near a large body of water, the other notable exception being the EWL viaducts leading to Tuas Link MRT (EW33). I believe these two places are the closest in Singapore you'll get to the typical "oceanside train views" you'd get from lines like the [Tokaido Main Line](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e86PRRAZIiU), [Enoden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mS6MX1rlhtc), or the [Nankai Main Line](https://youtu.be/nqm2WmvC194?si=1tNmorShYYGQK4N5&t=663).

Anyways, I digress. Let's continue on towards Bishan!

{% include video.html url='assets/bishan-trainspotting/at-grade-lentor-1-o.mp4' %}
**Figure 17:** At-grade Tracks Near Lentor

![Crossing the Mandai River]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/at-grade-lentor-2.jpg)
**Figure 18:** At-grade Tracks Near Lentor, Part 2

Shortly after we leave the Lower Seletar Reservoir, we enter an at-grade section of NSL tracks around the Lentor area. The at-grade tracks stretch on for quite a while and, in fact, the separation between Khatib (NS14) and Yio Chu Kang stations (NS15) is also unusually long. Rail Filmmaking has a [good video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_0i9BTaxvbg) about why these two stations are separated by quite a significant distance, including old plans for an entirely new station serving a proposed Disneyland alongside the Lower Seletar Reservoir.

As of 2026, this area is now mostly forested. It does make for some of the most scenic views in the MRT though!

Finally, we arrive at Bishan. Once again, the NSL enters an at-grade section as it transitions from elevated viaducts and underground tunnels. Caught in the middle is Bishan MRT (NS17).

The MRT network has been designed to be fully automated. To ensure this, the MRT must not have [level crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_crossing)---sections where the MRT intersects with roads and public footpaths. For this reason, MRT lines tend to follow *either elevated or underground alignments*. Bishan MRT station sits in the transition between these sections, explaining the unusual layout.

![Bishan Platform Screen Doors]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/bishan-1.jpg)
**Figure 20:** Bishan Platform Screen Doors

![Old Bishan Signs]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/bishan-2.jpg)
**Figure 21:** Old Bishan Signs

In fact, Bishan MRT is special in quite a lot of ways. It is one of the first MRT stations to be built. We can even see this in some of the old signs, such as the one above. The logo of the station shown in Figure 21 is an old design dating back to 2003, when the MRT was less than half of its current age. Contrast this with the modern, rounded design with a white border as seen Figure 20, and you can see hints of a station that has witnessed changes in a rapidly developing nation.

![Kawasaki C151B Entry]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/bishan-3.jpg)
**Figure 22:** A Southbound Kawasaki C151B Entering Bishan Station

To avoid inconveniencing the other commuters, I proceeded to take my photography outside. As I left, another train---a Kawasaki C151B---entered Bishan station heading south to Marina South Pier around a minute or so after the previous train left. This level of frequency is not unusual during rush hour. In fact, trainspotting either at the start of rush hour (around 5pm) or near the end of it (maybe around 8pm?) means that you get to see around twice as many trains within the same timeframe. I do recommend going somewhere between these two times. Just be careful not to inconvenience other commuters returning from their 9-to-5!

## Trainspotting Around Bishan

![Start of NSL Tunnels]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/bishan-trainspotting/trainspotting-1.jpg)
**Figure 25:** The Start of the North-South Line Tunnels

Walk a little south from the station, and you'll find a pedestrian footbridge. From here, you can see where the NSL first enters the underground tunnels heading towards the Central Business District (CBD). This spot is amazing for trainspotting! In fact, you'll find other trainspotters uploading [videos of trains found around these areas online](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZkkWvhVTOs).

At this point, I decided to go on a **scavenger hunt** for *all rolling stock* on the North-South and East-West Lines (both the NSL and EWL use the same rolling stock). As of September 2026, these are:

1. The 4th Generation **Kawasaki C151A**
1. The 5th Generation **Kawasaki C151B**
1. The 6th Generation **Kawasaki C151C**
1. The 7th Generation **Alstom R151**

I was [unfortunately one year late from seeing one of the first-generation C151 trains in revenue service](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ODhWnV70XE8), and by coincidence, I also [missed the scrapping of the last C151 train](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FUVfsTFsr-A) on the very same day I went trainspotting[^1]. Unfortunate!

Anyways, I allocated around 10-20 minutes or so to photograph some of these trains. Shown below are some select clips of various rolling stock entering and exiting Bishan MRT. See the appendix for the rest of the clips!

Near the end of my observation period, I spotted a Kawasaki C151C entering and a C151B leaving Bishan station simultaneously. I think you can clearly tell from the audio that I was foaming over this moment.

Actually, from this observation, you can tell that trains don't come at exact intervals down to the second. If there were an exact interval, then trains should leave and enter the station at exact, periodic moments. The fact that I only saw this happen once is proof that, although the MRT is automated, there seems to be some degree of variation in between waiting times[^2]. Quite interesting, but I digress!

[^2]: More specifically, it proves that if the train schedules exhibit some periodicity across time, that period should exceed the time between the arrival of two trains. For the math-inclined among you, one could arrive at this conclusion by modeling the state of the MRT network as some abstract function over the domain of time.

{% include video.html url='assets/bishan-trainspotting/trainspotting-12-o.mp4' %}
**Figure 30:** Kawasaki C151C and the Bishan MRT Northbound Platform

The cool thing about Bishan is that you can see the train enter the station from above, something you cannot do at any other station on the MRT network.

After seeing the two trains leave and enter simultaneously, I decided it was time for me to explore around Bishan.

As I walked around, I saw a sports centre and a section of the [park connector network (PCN)](https://pcn.nparks.gov.sg/). Near this PCN is the Bishan Depot. I would recommend visiting for train fans, but do note some important reminders:
1. The Bishan Depot is listed as a **Protected Area** under the [2017 Infrastructure Protection Act](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/IPA2017). This means that **you cannot take pictures or videos in or around the area of Bishan Depot**. Please make sure to respect these laws when you visit! The depot is critical national infrastructure and hence is for your eyes only. Do not post pictures or videos of it online. Do not describe key features of the depot to others either.
2. From experience, the PCN is rather busy with many joggers and cyclists. Some cyclists might not expect a pedestrian entering the PCN from the road next to the sports centre. Please do look left and right before entering the PCN. Head towards the seats alongside the PCN as soon as you can and be aware of cyclists and joggers behind and in front of you.

To be quite honest, this part of my trip was quite stressful for me[^3]. While I did my best to stay out of the way of cyclists, some of them do go quite fast. I'd advise you to be vigilant if you decide to visit this place.

[^3]: I don't like busy places and am not a sporty person. I also don't like confrontations.

## Getting to Bishan from the Central Business District

The route I took to get to Bishan is most likely not the path that tourists would take to get there, as I came from my classes in NTU. For tourists coming from the Central area, I recommend taking the North-South Line from a station like **Raffles Place** or **City Hall**. Do consult official maps if you're planning a trip here!

## Conclusion

Overall, Bishan MRT is one of the best spots for train fans in Singapore. Its unique position as the only at-grade MRT station enables hobbyist trainspotters to capture clips of MRT trains from many angles. The proximity of the Bishan Depot also makes it a worthwhile visit. Please do just remember not to take pictures or videos of the Bishan Depot and mind the cyclists and joggers on the nearby PCN.

I had quite a bit of fun visiting Bishan. Exploring the network that keeps Singapore moving brings me great joy. I hope it does so for you as well!
